`inverse-scale` multiplies a path by `at / value`. At the declared value the factor is 1. A larger value shrinks the path. Ordinary `scale` still grows. This is the kind for `distance` and for `margin`.

`projected-offset` moves the named paths opposite the camera shift. The gain comes from `cameraPose` and the inverse of `shiftEyeByPixels`. Without `depthRatio` the gain is `height / (2 * tan(fovY / 2))` pixels per unit, using the compiler field of view. With `depthRatio` the strafe is the translate itself, so the gain is that quantity divided by subject depth. The shift is not `value - at` pixels.

`reproject` replaces the baked subject and ground faces with the compiler camera at the new latitude or azimuth. The checker probe stays `+24`. On `camera-orbit` that probe drops `subject-f4` and `ground-f4` and adds `subject-f5` and `ground-f5`. The bind names every box face, including faces the probe drops and faces it adds. A plain path scale of the angle ratio does not match the compiler camera. A larger latitude does not shrink the outline by that ratio. Pixels outside the projected subject and ground stay put. The stage is not a target.
